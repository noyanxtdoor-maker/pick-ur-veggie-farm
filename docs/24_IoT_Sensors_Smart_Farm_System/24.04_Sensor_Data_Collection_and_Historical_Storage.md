# Sensor Data Collection and Historical Storage

## Purpose

IoT is valuable not only because it shows current conditions.

Its true power comes from storing historical data that allows Codex to discover patterns, predict problems, and improve farm decisions.

---

## Sensor Data Records

Every sensor reading stores:

- Device ID
- Sensor type
- Farm location
- Production zone
- Date and time
- Measurement value
- Measurement unit
- Device status
- Signal quality
- Battery level (if available)

---

## Data Frequency

Different sensors have different reading intervals.

Examples:

Temperature:
Every 5 minutes

Humidity:
Every 5 minutes

Water Level:
Every 15 minutes

EC:
Every 30 minutes

pH:
Every 30 minutes

---

## Historical Database

The ERP stores:

Short-term data:
High-detail readings

Long-term data:
Daily, weekly, and monthly summaries

---

## Data Analysis

Historical records allow Codex to identify:

- Temperature trends
- Seasonal changes
- Water consumption patterns
- Nutrient usage patterns
- Equipment performance decline

---

## Example Analysis

Question:

"Codex, why did lettuce yield decrease this month?"

Codex analyzes:

- Temperature history
- EC history
- Irrigation records
- Disease reports
- Labor activities

---

## Data Integrity

Sensor records cannot be manually modified.

If incorrect data exists:

- It is marked invalid
- The reason is recorded
- The original reading remains in history

---

## Data Retention Strategy

The system may archive old sensor records while maintaining important historical statistics.

---

## Principle

A single sensor reading is information.

Years of sensor history become intelligence.
