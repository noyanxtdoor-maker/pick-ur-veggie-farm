# Permission Matrix

## Purpose

The Permission Matrix defines exactly what every role in PickUrVeggie ERP V3 can and cannot do.

The system follows the principle:

"A user receives only the minimum access required to perform their job."

This protects:

- Company data
- Financial records
- Employee privacy
- Inventory integrity
- Customer information
- System security

---

# Role Philosophy

A role describes a person's responsibility.

A permission controls a specific action.

Example:

A person may have the role:

Supervisor

But may or may not have permission to:

- Approve payroll
- View salaries
- Adjust inventory
- Export financial reports

Permissions always have higher priority than role names.

---

# Core Roles

## 1. Developer

Purpose:

Technical owner of the ERP system.

Examples:

- System architect
- Internal developer
- CTO

---

Permissions:

Can:

- Access all modules
- Configure system settings
- Create new modules
- Manage databases
- Troubleshoot technical issues
- View audit logs
- Perform system maintenance

Restrictions:

- All actions are recorded in audit logs.
- Developer actions affecting financial data require justification.

---

## 2. Owner

Purpose:

Highest business authority.

Can:

- View all company information
- Approve high-level transactions
- Access financial reports
- Approve payroll
- Configure company policies
- Assign major roles

Restrictions:

- Cannot remove audit history.
- Cannot bypass financial integrity rules.

---

## 3. Co-Owner

Purpose:

Secondary business authority.

Can:

- Perform most owner functions
- Manage operations
- Approve transactions according to assigned authority

Restrictions:

- Certain strategic controls may remain owner-only.

Examples:

- Transfer ownership
- Remove owner account
- Change critical company identity settings

---

## 4. Administrator

Purpose:

Daily system management.

Can:

- Manage users
- Manage employees
- Assign roles
- Configure workflows
- Manage schedules
- View operational records

May access financial records only if explicitly granted.

---

Restrictions:

Cannot:

- Change company ownership
- Delete audit logs
- Perform unrestricted financial overrides

---

## 5. Accountant

Purpose:

Responsible for financial accuracy.

Can:

- View accounting records
- Create journal entries
- Manage receivables
- Manage payables
- Prepare payroll records
- Reconcile transactions
- Generate financial reports

---

Restrictions:

Cannot:

- Modify historical locked periods
- Delete approved financial records
- Change system security settings

---

## 6. Supervisor

Purpose:

Manages teams and daily farm operations.

Can:

- Assign tasks
- Monitor workers
- Review attendance corrections
- Approve operational tasks
- Record production activities
- View team performance

---

May have limited inventory authority:

Examples:

- Approve material usage
- Request stock adjustments

---

Restrictions:

Cannot:

- View company-wide financial reports
- View unauthorized employee salaries
- Modify accounting records

---

## 7. Operator

Purpose:

Skilled employee responsible for specific operations.

Examples:

- Greenhouse operator
- Irrigation operator
- Equipment operator
- Inventory operator

---

Can:

- Record assigned operations
- Update equipment records
- Record production activities
- Submit inventory requests
- Create reports

---

Restrictions:

Cannot:

- Approve high-level transactions
- Access sensitive financial data
- Change permissions

---

## 8. Worker

Purpose:

General field employee.

Can:

- Time in and time out
- View personal schedule
- View assigned tasks
- Record completed work
- Upload photos
- Submit requests
- Request leave
- Request cash advance
- View personal payroll information

---

Restrictions:

Cannot:

- View other employees' information
- Access inventory valuation
- View financial reports
- Approve transactions
- Change system settings

---

## 9. Future External Users

Examples:

- Customers
- Suppliers
- Consultants
- Auditors

---

Access is limited to information explicitly shared with them.

Examples:

Customer Portal:

Can:

- View their orders
- View invoices
- Check delivery status

Cannot:

- View other customers
- Access internal operations

---

Supplier Portal:

Can:

- View purchase orders
- Confirm deliveries

Cannot:

- View company financial data

---

# Permission Inheritance Rules

A higher role may receive broader access.

However:

Higher role does NOT mean unlimited power.

Examples:

Owner:

Cannot delete audit history.

Developer:

Cannot secretly modify accounting records.

Accountant:

Cannot access unrelated personal employee information without authorization.

---

# Temporary Permissions

The system supports temporary access.

Example:

A supervisor is assigned as acting manager for one week.

The ERP stores:

- Permission granted
- Start date
- End date
- Person who approved it
- Reason

After expiration:

The permission is automatically removed.

---

# Emergency Override System

Certain critical situations may require temporary override.

Examples:

- Inventory emergency
- System failure
- Urgent payroll correction

Requirements:

- Authorized role
- Reason for override
- Optional secondary approval
- Automatic audit logging

---

# Permission Audit Trail

Every sensitive action records:

- Who performed it
- What permission was used
- Which record was affected
- Date and time
- Device used
- Reason if required

---

# Zero Trust Security Principle

The system does not trust a user simply because they are inside the company.

Every important action requires:

- Correct identity
- Correct permission
- Valid device access
- Audit recording

---

# Final Permission Principle

The goal of security is not to prevent people from working.

The goal is to ensure every person has the correct level of access to perform their responsibilities safely.

A successful ERP gives the right information to the right person at the right time and prevents access to everyone else.
