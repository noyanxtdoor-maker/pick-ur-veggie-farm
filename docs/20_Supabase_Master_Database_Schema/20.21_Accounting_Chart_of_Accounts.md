# Accounting Chart of Accounts

The Chart of Accounts (COA) is the foundation of the financial system.

Every financial transaction must map to an account.

---

## Asset Accounts

Examples:

- Cash Drawer
- Petty Cash
- Bank Accounts
- GCash / Maya Wallets
- Accounts Receivable
- Seed Inventory
- Fertilizer Inventory
- Chemical Inventory
- Packaging Inventory
- Harvest Inventory
- Biological Assets
- Equipment & Machinery

---

## Liability Accounts

Examples:

- Accounts Payable
- Supplier Loans
- Bank Loans
- Employee Payables
- Taxes Payable

---

## Equity Accounts

Examples:

- Owner Capital
- Retained Earnings
- Current Year Profit/Loss

---

## Income Accounts

Examples:

- Retail Vegetable Sales
- Wholesale Sales
- Delivery Charges
- Other Agricultural Income

---

## Expense Accounts

Examples:

- Labor
- Fuel
- Electricity
- Repairs
- Equipment Maintenance
- Office Expenses
- Spoilage Loss
- Disaster Loss

---

## Agricultural Rule

Expenses are not automatically recorded when materials are purchased.

Example:

Purchase fertilizer:

Inventory Asset increases.

Only when fertilizer is consumed:

Inventory decreases.
Crop production cost increases.
