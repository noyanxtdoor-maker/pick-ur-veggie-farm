# Tax, Regulatory and Compliance

## Purpose

The ERP should maintain records required for legal compliance and government reporting.

The system stores financial evidence and generates reports, but compliance rules must remain configurable according to local laws and future changes.

---

## Tax Categories

The system may classify transactions by:

- Taxable sales
- Tax-exempt sales
- Zero-rated transactions
- Taxable purchases
- Non-taxable purchases

---

## Supporting Documents

Examples:

Sales:

- Sales invoices
- Official receipts
- Customer records

Purchases:

- Supplier invoices
- Purchase receipts
- Delivery documents

Expenses:

- Expense receipts
- Approval records
- Supporting evidence

---

## Government Contributions

Payroll records may support:

- Employee deductions
- Employer contributions
- Government reporting

Examples include:

- Social security contributions
- Health insurance contributions
- Employee savings programs

---

## Tax Reporting

The ERP may generate reports such as:

- Sales summaries
- Purchase summaries
- Expense reports
- Payroll summaries
- Asset schedules

---

## Compliance Audit Trail

Every financial document must maintain:

- Creation date
- Creator
- Approval history
- Modification history
- Supporting attachments

---

## Compliance Principle

The ERP must be flexible.

Tax laws change over time.

Business rules should be configurable without rebuilding the entire system.
