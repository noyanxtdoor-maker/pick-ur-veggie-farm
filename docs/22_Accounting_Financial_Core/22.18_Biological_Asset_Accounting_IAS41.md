# Biological Asset Accounting (IAS 41)

## Purpose

Continuous harvesting crops require special accounting treatment.

Examples:

- Eggplant
- Tomato
- Chili
- Bell pepper

These crops produce revenue over several months while their establishment costs occur early in the cycle.

---

## Pre-Harvest Phase


Costs accumulated:

- Nursery operations
- Seed costs
- Transplant labor
- Initial fertilization
- Irrigation setup
- Trellising

Accounting classification:

Crop Work-In-Progress (WIP)

---

## Productive Stage

Once the crop reaches production maturity:

Field Crop WIP

        ↓

Productive Biological Asset


The accumulated cost becomes a biological asset.

---

## Continuous Harvest Depletion Model


Example:

Productive Biological Asset:

₱120,000


Expected lifetime yield:

10,000 kg


Initial cost rate:

₱12/kg


Week 1 harvest:

500 kg


Cost released:

500 × ₱12 = ₱6,000


Accounting Entry:


Debit:

Harvest Inventory      ₱6,000


Credit:

Biological Asset       ₱6,000


---

## Rolling Yield Adjustment

The ERP continuously updates expected remaining yield.

Examples:

- Pest damage
- Weather changes
- Exceptional production


This prevents:

- Artificially low early costs
- Extreme cost spikes at the end of the crop cycle


---

## Abnormal Biological Loss

Examples:

- Typhoon
- Major disease outbreak
- Irrigation system failure


The ERP records:

Loss Expense

        ↑

Removed from Biological Asset


The cost is not unfairly transferred to surviving plants.


---

## Crop Termination

When a crop reaches the end of its economic life:

Remaining asset value is evaluated.

Possible outcomes:

- Fully depleted
- Written off as loss
- Offset by recovered materials


The entire crop lifecycle remains permanently recorded.
